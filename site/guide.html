PROJECT NOTES / FIRST DRAFT

A Bitcoin-rooted identity.
Held by its owner.

Why Open2Artist exists, what its self-custodial protocol proposes, and what the first experiment must prove.

01 / PURPOSE

Make music identity self-custodial.

O2A is a proposed decentralized Bitcoin protocol in which artists, venues, promoters, labels or organizations, live events, and albums each have their own public-key-rooted EntityID.

A dedicated BIP340 identity key remains in the owner's wallet and is separate from keys that spend bitcoin. Rotatable controller keys handle day-to-day signing. RGB client-side state anchored to Bitcoin records genesis, controller changes, recovery-policy changes, and revocation.

The owner controls the cryptographic ID. A spoken name is a non-exclusive claim supported by DNS, social, and peer evidence. Wallets show competing claims and Bitcoin-established chronology rather than awarding a permanent global username.

The goal: the same valid identity history, evidence, policy, protocol version, and evaluation context should produce the same result in independent wallets.

02 / THE PROBLEM

Many records. No owner-controlled root.

A world-class superstar may have different artist IDs on Spotify, Apple Music, and other services, plus separate metadata records in MusicBrainz, Wikidata, or ISNI. These references belong to different namespaces, and mapping them does not prove the artist authorized that mapping.

O2A adds an owner-controlled cryptographic root rather than another platform account. Platform and community identifiers remain useful as signed references and evidence.

The same problem applies to venue operators, promoters, labels, organizers, live events, and albums. A physical venue is distinct from its operator; a festival brand is distinct from an individual event; an album is a work with its own ID held by its custodian.

  • Ownership: a platform controls its identifier namespace; the participant needs a key-rooted ID it can hold.
  • Ambiguity: identical names and changing roles can merge unrelated histories.
  • Fragmentation: evidence from one service may be difficult to inspect and reuse elsewhere.
  • Continuity: working keys and representatives change while the root identity and history must remain checkable.
  • Disagreement: competing claims and corrections need an explicit, durable record.

O2A aims to complement useful identifiers and standards. Its value comes from self custody, explicit authorization, and reusable evidence—not merely adding another number.

03 / THE SOLUTION

Separate identity state, evidence, and recognition.

  1. Identity state: Bitcoin establishes order and seals; RGB validates the supplied EntityID lifecycle and controller authority.
  2. Evidence: signed name claims, DNS or social control proofs, independent attestations, content hashes, and challenges.
  3. Recognition: a named, versioned policy evaluates a specified evidence set and explains its result.

The first scenario creates separate key-rooted IDs for a generic artist, venue, promoter, and event. The event key signs its canonical manifest hash; participants sign scoped relationship evidence. The same pattern gives an album its own ID and manifest hash held by its custodian.

Bitcoin does not decide who is the real artist, and no privileged node set votes on it. Each wallet validates the cryptographic history and evaluates the same preserved external evidence under the same policy.

A signature establishes authorship and integrity. It does not establish that a performance occurred, that a participant exclusively owns a spoken name, or that several signers are independent. Policies must account for coordinated fake identities, conflicts, and missing evidence.

Registries are rebuildable projections over evidence. Destroying a search index should not destroy an independently retained history or alter verification of the same package.

04 / ARTIST VALUE

Signed approval with direct remuneration.

A proposed application lets an artist approve a particular use of profile, brand, catalog, or event material and receive payment at the authorization step. Examples include a commercial tour card, an approved promotional mention, or a maintained catalog feed.

A “metalink” here means a link accompanied by signed metadata describing the material, permitted use, terms, and authorization. The participant can choose the price, scope, duration, and payment destination. Offers can also be free.

  1. The artist or an authorized representative signs a specific offer.
  2. The publisher accepts the terms and pays an invoice tied to that offer.
  3. When payment and delivery both succeed, the publisher receives a signed permission grant associated with the payment receipt.
  4. A verifier checks that the use is within the grant's scope.

Sats over Lightning are the first payment candidate to test. A later, compatible swap adapter could allow receipt of a supported USDt asset. RGB contracts could represent selected digital rights where their state-transition rules are useful.

This is an opt-in relationship between participants. O2A cannot charge unrelated websites for public mentions or establish exclusive rights merely because someone controls an identity key.

Payment received, permission delivered, and optional asset conversion are distinct outcomes. A production implementation would need restart recovery, idempotent processing, and an explicit remedy when payment succeeds but delivery fails. Immediate completion, artist income, and adoption are hypotheses to test.

05 / TECHNOLOGY

Use each layer for what it can prove.

Bitcoin, BIP340, and RGB

The immutable secp256k1/BIP340 root identifies the EntityID and signs genesis; current RGB state determines controller authority. The root is not automatically a permanent controller and is never a bitcoin spending key. Bitcoin provides ordered anchors and single-use seals. RGB clients validate the supplied off-chain identity state for creation, controller rotation, recovery-policy change, and revocation. Ordinary profile edits and external observations do not each require a transaction.

Self-custodial wallet / node

The minimum client stores the seed, root and controller keys, RGB consignments, attestations, and proof packages on the user's machine. It can use a full Bitcoin node or a clearly labeled light mode. Online it collects fresh DNS and social observations; offline it validates packages already retained.

Discovery and publication

Pubky / PKARR can publish a public profile through an Ed25519 key explicitly bound by the O2A identity. Nostr is an optional signed-publication adapter. DNS and social channels provide control evidence. A signed locator advertises a content-addressed public identity proof package that another wallet validates locally. None replaces the Bitcoin identity root, and private wallet state stays out of public storage.

Policy and applications

Canonical encoding, separate signing domains, and a named policy make preserved evidence independently reproducible. Lightning payments, USDt swaps, optional verification bonds, and additional RGB rights profiles are downstream mechanisms with separate acceptance gates.

The stack figure keeps PostgreSQL and Redis outside the authority boundary while showing Bitcoin/RGB as required identity infrastructure. The planned reference wallet and node are intended to be open-source software anyone can inspect, build, and run; a source license must be selected before implementation begins.

06 / WHY NOW

A current concern. A bounded experiment.

Music identity and authorization are active industry concerns. Bitcoin, RGB client-side validation, public-key discovery, and existing identifier standards provide components to test without appointing a central name registrar.

The first proposition is deliberately narrow: can separate self-custodial wallets create, rotate, recover, and revoke music EntityIDs, exchange portable evidence, and independently reproduce the same policy result? A later paid-use experiment can test direct remuneration separately.

Success includes usable key recovery, understandable competing claims, reliable consignment exchange, explicit full-node and light-mode security differences, and willingness to publish evidence. A technically correct demonstration alone does not establish adoption.

07 / STATUS

A specification awaiting implementation.

As of September 2026, O2A is in design and specification. The Bitcoin/RGB identity profile, schemas, canonical serialization, controller transitions, and conformance vectors precede a production implementation. This site describes the proposal and includes a teaching simulation, not a working wallet, verifier, or payment service.

The initial focus is the self-custodial identity lifecycle, portable evidence, and deterministic verification. Public-profile discovery, paid permissions, GatePass ticketing, SplitNight allocations, additional rights profiles, and settlement adapters are downstream work with separate acceptance gates.

The release pipeline is a design target: local Bitcoin/RGB regtest, lifecycle conformance, independent-wallet exchange, public testnet, public beta, then a mainnet identity release. Those environments are not running.

All artist examples are generic. No real artist names, account IDs, endorsements, or customer claims are used. This static draft has no account creation, analytics, wallet connection, or payment collection.
